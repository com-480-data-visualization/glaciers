Swiss Glacier Retreat

GLAMOS inventory outlines · 1850 to SGI2023

See how Swiss glaciers retreated, glacier by glacier.

This project turns historical glacier inventories into an interactive story about uneven retreat. It shows where ice surface has disappeared since 1850, why large glaciers can dominate absolute losses, and why smaller or more accessible glaciers can feel especially dramatic in familiar Alpine tourism landscapes.

See story
01

Make retreat visible

The map compares measured inventory outlines from 1850, 1931, 1973, 2010, 2016, and SGI2023. The orange footprint makes the lost ice surface visible instead of reducing retreat to one number.

02

Compare different kinds of loss

Aletsch and Gorner show large absolute area losses. Rhone shows how a smaller, highly visited glacier can transform a familiar landscape even when its total area is much lower.

03

Separate outlines from context

SGI2023 / 2021–2024 imagery is the latest outline inventory used for polygon area. 2025 GLAMOS files provide velocity, mass-balance, and volume context, not new glacier outlines.

What the story follows

The guided map starts in the Valais and Bernese Alps, then steps through Aletsch, Gorner, and Rhone from the oldest inventory to the latest loaded inventory. You can also leave the story and explore every SGI2023 glacier directly.

What you can inspect

Drag or play the area timeline, click any glacier for an area chart, switch to measured flow velocity, and open the detailed pages for the three featured glaciers.
